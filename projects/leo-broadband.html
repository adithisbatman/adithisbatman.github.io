LEO Broadband Market Prioritization

A percentile-based scoring model that ranks UK grid cells to identify the strongest candidates for an initial satellite broadband launch.

Excel Scoring models Regression Market analytics
~900UK grid cells analyzed
10Cells selected for beta
50Maximum customers per cell
500Maximum customers across 10 cells

01 / The Problem

A low-Earth-orbit satellite broadband provider is preparing a limited UK beta launch. Service can only be activated in a limited share of grid cells, with a geographic eligibility cutoff and a maximum of 50 customers per cell.

The question: Which 10 cells should launch first to maximize potential customer acquisition?

I analyzed broadband availability, population, sign-up leads, and latitude using UK government broadband data.

02 / Methodology

Attempt 1: Hard Cutoffs

I initially filtered cells by latitude, population, leads, and broadband availability. Population and leads were heavily right-skewed, so I used medians for those variables and means for broadband metrics. This produced eight candidates.

The limitation: binary thresholds excluded otherwise attractive markets that narrowly missed one requirement.

Attempt 2: Continuous Suitability Score

I replaced binary cutoffs with a weighted percentile-based score, ranking every eligible market across multiple dimensions.

DimensionWhat it capturesIdeal characteristic
DemandPopulation and leadsLarge market with demonstrated interest
Unmet needLimited access to adequate broadbandHigh need for an alternative
CompetitionFibre, wireless, and broadband coverageFewer competitive substitutes
Interest intensityLeads relative to populationStrong interest for market size

Regression Diagnostic

I regressed leads on population. All 10 selected cells had positive residuals, meaning they generated more leads than population alone predicted. This was a supporting diagnostic, not proof of paid conversion.

03 / Suitability Score

Each variable was converted to a percentile rank to reduce the influence of skewed raw values. Competition variables were inverted so greater competition lowered the score.

Score = 100 × [0.15P + 0.15L + 0.25UR + 0.07(1 − avg(F,M)) + 0.04(1 − W) + 0.14(1 − C) + 0.20LR]
FactorWeightWhy it matters
UR · Unable to get 30 Mbit/s25%Strong unmet broadband need
LR · Lead ratio20%Interest relative to population
P · Population15%Potential market size
L · Leads15%Existing demonstrated interest
C · 30–300 Mbit/s coverage14%Direct broadband alternatives (inverted)
F, M · Full fibre and ≥300 Mbit/s7%High-speed alternatives (inverted)
W · Fixed wireless access4%Additional competitive substitute (inverted)

Weights total 100%. Each letter represents a percentile-normalized input. Competition inputs are inverted.

05 / What I Would Do Next

Additional Data

  • Historical lead-to-subscriber conversion
  • Income and affordability
  • Competitor pricing and performance
  • Housing and terrain characteristics
  • Geographic clustering

Post-Launch Metrics

  • Sign-up funnel conversion
  • Time to reach customer capacity
  • Subscriber churn
  • Connection speed and reliability
  • Customer acquisition cost

The Suitability Score is a hypothesis rather than a proven predictor. With actual launch data, I would evaluate which characteristics best predict paid conversion, test the sensitivity of the weights, and update market rankings accordingly.
